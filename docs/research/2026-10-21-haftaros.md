# Haftaros and Pirkei Avos: references, customs, precedence (research note, 2026-10-21)

Scope: the haftara reference for each of the 54 parshiyos and the combined parshiyos, for Ashkenaz,
Sephardi, Chabad and (where a public source exists) Teimani (Baladi). It also covers the special
haftaros, the precedence rules when occasions coincide, and the summer Pirkei Avos cycle. The
machine-readable data is in the companion `haftaros.json` file. Everything here is a reference only, with
no text. Clean-room: no hebcal, KosherJava, kosher-zmanim or Calendrical Calculations code or data
was opened.

Method: every parsha was cross-checked against at least two independent sources:
(a) the Sefaria calendar API, swept every Shabbos from 2022-09-03 to 2027-10-02 with
`custom=ashkenazi` and `custom=sephardi`; (b) English Wikipedia "Haftara", which compiles Hertz,
ArtScroll, Hirsch, Koren, Cassuto, Jerusalem Crown and others; (c) Hebrew Wikipedia "הפטרה", whose
symbol list includes Chabad (חבד) and Teimani (ת); (d) AlHaTorah's haftarot index (R. Eli Duker),
which footnotes manuscripts and the Chabad "ספר ההפטרות"; (e) jewfaq.org parsha pages, which give
Ashkenaz and Sephardi; (f) Chabad.org, both its own "List of Haftorah Readings where Customs Vary"
(based on Siddur im Dach / Torah Or) and its "Haftarah Companion" pages. The halachic rules come from
the Shulchan Aruch, Rema and Mishna Berura texts on Sefaria, and from Megillah 31a.

Reference format: `Book c:v-c:v`, with multiple ranges joined by `; ` and the book repeated.
`Isaiah 66:1-24; Isaiah 66:23` means "read the chapter, then repeat verse 23".

The Acharei-Kedoshim exception: Rema OC 428:8 (citing Sefer Minhagim) has Ashkenazim read "Halo

report the Rema's practice as universal (both as cited by MB; not opened). MB 428:26 (citing R. Akiva Eiger) adds that if Acharei

The three haftaros of affliction and seven of consolation (SA OC 428:8; Pesikta origin (unverified)) are:
Jer 1:1-2:3, Jer 2:4-28 + 3:4 (S/C: + 4:1-2), Isa 1:1-27; then Isa 40:1-26, 49:14-51:3,
54:11-55:5, 51:12-52:12, 54:1-10, 60:1-22, 61:10-63:9 (Vaeschanan to Nitzavim). The Teimani
affliction set is Jer 1:1-19, Isa 1:1-20, Isa 1:21-31.

Shabbos HaGadol timing (AlHaTorah, which compiles the sources; the primary works it cites were not opened, so they are (unverified)): Ashkenaz has three customs.
(1) Read it every year: Maharil, Tiferes Adam, most Chassidic courts listed, and Teimani Shami.
(2) Read it only when Erev Pesach is Shabbos: SA HaRav 430:3, Be'er Heitev 430:1, Kitzur SA, Belz,
Chabad, Frankfurt, Pressburg. (3) Gra / Lithuania: every year EXCEPT when Erev Pesach is Shabbos.
Baladi Teimanim never read it. The Chabad.org list confirms that Chabad reads it only when Shabbos
HaGadol is Erev Pesach. A library should expose this as a per-community option, defaulting to
"every year".

## 3. Precedence rules

1. **Shabbos Rosh Chodesh** reads Isa 66, overriding the parsha (SA 425:1). MB 425:4: if
   forgotten, read Isa 66 after the parsha haftara.
2. **Rosh Chodesh Teves** always falls in Chanukah, so the Chanukah haftara is read (SA 684:3,
   MB 684:11).
3. **Rosh Chodesh Adar on Shekalim** reads the Shekalim haftara (MB 685:3: the Torah reading ended
   with Shekalim). **Rosh Chodesh Nisan on HaChodesh** reads the HaChodesh haftara (MB 685:13).
   Chabad, and some Sephardim per Dotan (via en.wiki, unverified), append the first and last verses of Isa 66, or of I Sam 20
   when it is Erev Rosh Chodesh.
4. **Rosh Chodesh Elul on Re'eh**: the Mechaber reads Re'eh's own 54:11-55:5 so as not to break
   the seven of consolation (MB 425:5). The Rema (Ashkenaz) and Chabad read Isa 66, because it also
   contains consolation (MB 425:6). Ki Seitzei then reads 54:1-10 followed by 54:11-55:5 (Ashkenaz,
   per en.wikipedia; Chabad.org: "a short passage from Re'eh").
5. **Rosh Chodesh Av on Shabbos** (Masei or Matos-Masei): the Rema cites both Shim'u and Isa 66,
   calling Isa 66 "ikar where there is no custom". MB 425:8 notes that the Gra rejected this and
   ruled Shim'u. Chabad reads Shim'u. Recommended default: Shim'u.
6. **Machar Chodesh never displaces** Shim'u or Aniya So'ara (Rema 425:2), the four parshiyos or
   Chanukah. Chabad: for Re'eh on Erev Rosh Chodesh Elul, read Re'eh's haftara plus I Sam 20:18 and
   20:42. Machar Chodesh Tishrei cannot arise, since 29 Elul is Erev Rosh Hashana.
7. **Rosh Chodesh on Shabbos and Sunday**: Isa 66 only (Rema, because one does not skip between
   prophets). The Mechaber and Chabad append I Sam 20:18 and 20:42.
8. **Combined parshiyos** use the second parsha's haftara, except Acharei-Kedoshim (Ashkenaz/Chabad
   read Amos 9) and Nitzavim-Vayelech (Nitzavim's, the 7th consolation).
9. **Pinchas**: if it falls on or after 17 Tammuz (the usual case), read Jer 1:1-2:3. Its own
   haftara (I Kgs 18:46-19:21) applies only when it falls before 17 Tammuz. Separate Matos then
   reads Jer 1:1-2:3, separate Masei reads Shim'u, and Matos-Masei reads Shim'u.
10. **Shuva** is on the Shabbos between Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur. When Rosh Hashana is Mon-Tue,
    Haazinu falls after Yom Kippur and reads II Sam 22. The Rema follows the first opinion, against
    swapping Dirshu and Shuva (SA 428:8).
11. **Chassan** (Sos Asis) never displaces any of the above (Rema 428:8; MB 684:8).
12. **Israel** has no second Yom Tov days. Shemini Atzeres reads Josh 1, and the parsha
    divergence after Pesach/Shavuos only changes which haftara lands on a date.

## 4. Pirkei Avos

- Basis: Rema OC 292:2, "we say Pirkei Avos in the summer and Shir HaMaalos in the winter, each
  place according to its custom". The Mishna Berura on 292 (Sefaria text) has no Avos-specific
  comment beyond MB 292:9 (Magen Avraham): if the derasha runs to dusk, skip the chapter. Kaf
  HaChaim 292:22-24 supplies the reasons and customs.
- Start: the first Shabbos after Pesach. One chapter per week, 1 to 6 in order, cycling. Chapter 6
  (Kinyan Torah) is a baraita added so that six weeks cover the six Shabbosim of the Omer. It
  lands on the Shabbos before Shavuos (en.wikipedia; Jewish Press).
- End:
  - (a) Until Shavuos only: Sephardi practice today, per Kaf HaChaim 292:23.
  - (b) Until Rosh Hashana: most communities (OU, citing Machzor Vitry 143 (unverified)). The cycle
    runs about four times, recalling Moshe teaching the Torah four times (Me'am Loez, via OU, unverified).
  - (c) Other: Pesach to the Shabbos before 17 Tammuz; after Shavuos to Masei at two chapters a
    week. Some Mizrachi communities have no recorded custom (OU).
- Skipped: when Erev Tisha B'Av is Shabbos (Rema OC 553:2), and on Shavuos that falls on Shabbos
  (OU).
- End of season: chapters are doubled in Elul so that the last Shabbos before Rosh Hashana reads
  chapters 5-6 (Jewish Press, R. Klass). One East-European schedule has 15 Shabbosim: 12 single,
  then the last 3 double. The sources fetched do not specify which weeks before the last are
  doubled. A common printed pattern doubles backward from the end (..., 3-4, 5-6).

## 5. Disagreements found

- **Acharei Mos (A)**: ends 22:19 (Hertz, Sefaria, jewfaq) or 22:16 (Koren, Cassuto, he.wiki).
  **Kedoshim (A)**: Amos 9 (most) or Ezek 22 (ArtScroll/Hirsch, when Acharei read Amos).
  **Kedoshim (S)**: begins 20:2 (most) or 20:1 (Hirsch, Benisch).
- **Vayetze (A)**: some sources add Joel 2:26-27 (he.wiki, MB 428:22) and some do not (Sefaria,
  jewfaq). Sephardi variant: Hos 11:7-13:5 (ArtScroll S).
- **Vayishlach (A)**: older chumashim have Hos 11:7-12:12. MB 428:22 calls that an error and
  follows the Gra's Obadiah.
- **Matos-Masei / Masei (S)**: Sefaria gives Jer 2:4-28 + 3:4 for both customs. Both Wikipedias
  and Chabad.org give S = + 4:1-2.
- **Shavuos 2 (A)**: SA 494:2 and en.wiki/Chabad.org have 2:20-3:19. Sefaria, he.wiki and
  AlHaTorah ("most Ashkenazi chumashim") have 3:1-19.
- **Pesach 1**: A is 5:2-6:1 (+6:27?) or 3:5-7 + 5:2-6:1 + 6:27. AlHaTorah says Chabad ends at
  6:1, but Chabad.org itself gives 3:5-7; 5:2-6:1; 6:27. Followed Chabad.org.
- **Shabbos Chol HaMoed Pesach (A/S)**: en.wiki 37:1-17; everyone else 37:1-14.
- **Rosh Hashana 1**: en.wiki "1:1-2:20" is a typo (all others 2:10).
- **Tisha B'Av mincha (A)**: he.wiki ends 56:6; others 56:8.
- **HaChodesh (C)**: en.wiki 45:18-46:16; Chabad.org companion and AlHaTorah 46:15. The Chabad
  sefer notes the Rebbe added 45:16-17 and 46:16-18 personally, "not a ruling for the public".
- **Bereshis (C)**: Chabad sefer note: in a leap year the Rebbe read to 43:10, not a public custom
  (AlHaTorah fn.).
- **Teimani**: Ki Sisa end 18:45 (he.wiki) vs 18:46 (en.wiki). Masei is Isa 1:1-20 (he.wiki,
  AlHaTorah fn.) vs = Sephardi (en.wiki; Shami practice). Matos alone: Jer 1:1-19 (he) vs 1:1-2:3
  (en). Sukkos 2 ends 8:21 (he) vs 8:16 (en). Tisha B'Av mincha: Hosea only (he, AlHaTorah) vs +
  Micah (en).
- **Sefaria calendar data quirk**: its `sephardi` feed misses Rosh Chodesh, Machar Chodesh and
  four-parshiyos overrides whenever the Sephardi weekly haftara differs from the Ashkenazi one, and
  falls back to the plain weekly haftara. Examples: Bereshis 2023-10-14 (Machar Chodesh), Noach
  2024-11-02 (RC), Vayakhel 2025-03-22 (Parah), Shemini 2027-04-03 (HaChodesh), Kedoshim
  2027-05-08 (RC). It also once lists S HaChodesh as 45:16-46:18. Do not use it as a golden
  oracle for Sephardi special Shabbosim.
- jewfaq.org's ranges look format-identical to a well-known open-source calendar's output, so it
  may not be fully independent of Sefaria/Wikipedia lineages. It was used only as a third
  confirmation.
