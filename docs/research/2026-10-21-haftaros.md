# Haftaros by minhag: research notes

- Date: 2026-10-21
- Purpose: Background facts for Luach's haftara feature. This is a list of the prophetic readings (Bible chapter:verse references) for every parsha, every joined parsha pair, the special Shabbosos and the holidays, according to Ashkenaz, Sephardi, Chabad and Teimani (Yemenite) custom. It also records which reading wins when two occasions coincide.
- Output data: `data-staging/haftaros.json` (keys `parsha` and `special`; each entry has `ashkenaz`, `sephardi`, `chabad`, `teimani`, `notes`).
- Clean-room statement: **no code, data files or data structures from any other calendar or zmanim library were opened, copied or consulted.** Only verse references (facts) were collected, from the public sources listed below, and each value was cross-checked.

## Method

1. Collected candidate references from two encyclopedic lists that were compiled independently: English Wikipedia "Haftara" and Hebrew Wikipedia "הפטרה". Both were read as raw wikitext, and both break readings out by community.
2. Cross-checked each value against a scholarly list, Rabbi Eli Duker's haftara survey on AlHaTorah.org. It cites manuscripts, early authorities and modern community customs, and names Teimani (Baladi/Shami) and Chabad practice explicitly.
3. Cross-checked Ashkenaz and Sephardi values against Sefaria's public calendar API. It was queried for every Shabbos from 2024-01-06 to 2036-12-27 with `custom=ashkenazi` and `custom=sephardi` (1,356 responses). Sefaria serves as a cross-check only. Its Sephardi data is incomplete: it gives the Ashkenazi `Jeremiah 2:4-28; 3:4` for Sephardi Masei, and it mixes up some special Shabbosos. It also leaves out the repeated verse in Isaiah 66.
4. Verified Chabad values directly against Chabad's own published dated haftara pages. Each page shows the haftara Chabad reads on a given date (its own note: "These Haftarah texts follow Chabad custom"). It was fetched for about 55 dated occasions chosen to isolate each parsha and each coincidence rule. Chabad's own list of where it differs, Sefer HaMinhagim's "List of Haftorah Readings where Customs Vary", was used to decide which entries needed checking.
5. Took precedence rules from the Shulchan Arukh, Orach Chayim 425, 428, 684 and 685 (with Rema) and from the Mishnah Berurah (read on Sefaria), plus Sefer HaMinhagim for Chabad.
6. Acceptance rule: a value was recorded only when at least two sources agree and no source of equal weight contradicts it. Where sources conflict, the value is `null` and the candidates are listed in `notes`. This applies mostly to Teimani. For Chabad, a dated page published by Chabad outranks secondary lists.

"Teimani" here means the Baladi (Rambam-based) rite, wherever the sources separate it from Shami, which often follows Sephardi practice. Shami variants are given in `notes`.

## Coverage

- Parsha entries (54 single parshiyos plus 7 joined pairs = 61). Ashkenaz, Sephardi and Chabad are complete. Teimani is complete except for these `null` entries: chayei-sarah, ki-sisa, masei, acharei-mos-kedoshim, behar-bechukosai, matos-masei.
- Special entries: 48. Sephardi is `null` only for fast-day-mincha, because most Sephardim read no haftara then. Teimani is `null` for the coincidence keys (no Yemenite source found) and for shabbos-hagadol (Baladi read none), affliction-2, sukkos-2, shavuos-2, tisha-bav-mincha and fast-day-mincha (no haftara).
- The `chabad` field always holds an explicit reference, never "same". This keeps consumers simple, and Chabad differs from Ashkenaz in about 20 places.

## Disagreements inside one minhag (recorded in `notes`)

- **Acharei Mos / Kedoshim (Ashkenaz).** The JSON follows the Rema (SA OC 428:8, which names Amos 9:7 as "the haftara of Acharei Mos"), ArtScroll, a published Ashkenazi luach (per AHT) and Chabad (CHB): Acharei Mos = Amos 9:7-15, Kedoshim = Ezekiel 22:1-16. Hertz, Koren, Sefaria and others swap them: Acharei Mos = Ezekiel 22:1-16 (or 1-19), Kedoshim = Amos. Following the Vilna Gaon, some read Amos on both weeks to avoid Ezekiel 22.
- **Vayetzei (Ashkenaz).** Many add Joel 2:26-27 after Hosea 14:10 (Mishnah Berurah 428 per AHT).
- **Vayishlach (Ashkenaz).** A minority read Hosea 11:7-12:12 instead of Obadiah.
- **Shabbos Shuva (Ashkenaz).** The base is Hosea + Joel 2:15-27. Many also add Micah 7:18-20, and some start Joel at 2:11.
- **Shavuos II (Ashkenaz).** Habakkuk 2:20-3:19 (OU, Chabad, the Yetziv Pisgam practice) or 3:1-19 (HeWP, SEF, AHT "most Ashkenazi chumashim").
- **Pesach I (Ashkenaz).** Joshua 3:5-7 is prefixed by Chabad and some Ashkenazim (OU calls it optional). Others read 5:2-6:1 and 6:27 only, or 5:2-6:1 alone.
- **Shabbos Chol HaMoed Pesach.** EnWP gives Ezekiel 37:1-17 for A/S, against 37:1-14 in HeWP, AHT, SEF and CHB.
- **Rosh Hashana I.** EnWP's "A, S: I Samuel 1:1-2:20" is an outlier; every other source has 1:1-2:10.

## Precedence rules (when occasions coincide)

General order, from the sources:

1. **Holiday or Chol HaMoed haftaros** replace the weekly one.
2. **The four parshiyos** (Shekalim, Zachor, Parah, HaChodesh) beat Rosh Chodesh and Machar Chodesh. Rema OC 425:1: "if Rosh Chodesh falls on one of the four parshiyos, read the parsha's haftara". SA OC 685:1: Shekalim on Rosh Chodesh Adar reads "Vayichros Yehoyada". EnWP: HaChodesh beats Rosh Chodesh Nisan.
   - Chabad adds the first and last verse(s) of the displaced haftara: Isaiah 66:1, 66:23-24, 66:23 for Rosh Chodesh, or I Samuel 20:18 and 20:42 for Machar Chodesh. Sources: SHM; CHB 2025-03-01, 2028-02-26, 2022-04-02.
   - Dotan (via EnWP) reports that Sephardim/Mizrachim add the same verses on HaChodesh. This is a single source, so it is not encoded.
3. **Chanukah** beats Rosh Chodesh Teves: read "Roni v'simchi" (SA OC 684:3; MB 684:11, publicising the miracle comes first). Chanukah also beats Machar Chodesh, since EnWP lists Teves among the months whose eve gets no Machar Chodesh. Chabad adds the Rosh Chodesh verses, and the Machar Chodesh verses on 30 Kislev (CHB 2022-12-24, 2015-12-12).
4. **Shabbos Rosh Chodesh** beats the weekly haftara and Machar Chodesh (SA OC 425:1).
   - If Rosh Chodesh is Shabbos and Sunday, SA 425:2 adds the first and last verse of Machar Chodesh after Isaiah 66. Sephardi and Chabad (SHM) do this.
   - The Rema (and MB 425:11) reads Isaiah 66 alone: "one does not skip from prophet to prophet".
5. **Three of affliction and seven of consolation** (SA OC 428:8):
   - **Rosh Chodesh Av** (Masei/Matos-Masei on 1 Av). The Rema cites both "Shim'u" and Isaiah 66 (the latter "primary where there is no custom"). MB 425:8: the Vilna Gaon rules Shim'u, and EnWP says most communities read Shim'u. Chabad reads Shim'u plus Isaiah 66:1, 66:23-24, 66:23 (SHM, quoting the Rebbe Rashab; CHB 2025-07-26).
   - **Rosh Chodesh Elul on Shabbos Re'eh.** The SA reads Re'eh's "Aniya So'arah" so the seven are not broken up (Sephardi). The Rema says the custom is Isaiah 66 (Ashkenaz, Chabad), and then Ki Seitzei reads 54:1-55:5 to make up the skipped haftara (MB 425:7; CHB 2029-08-25; EnWP).
   - **Erev Rosh Chodesh Elul on Shabbos Re'eh.** Machar Chodesh does not displace Aniya So'arah (SA OC 425:2 gloss). Chabad appends I Samuel 20:18 and 20:42 (CHB 2025-08-23); Frankfurt read Machar Chodesh (EnWP).
   - Rosh Chodesh Av can never fall on Sunday (MB 425:9), so Machar Chodesh never meets Shim'u.
6. **Shabbos Shuva** always reads "Shuva", whichever parsha it falls on (SA OC 428:8). When Rosh Hashana falls on Monday or Tuesday, Haazinu comes after Yom Kippur and reads II Samuel 22 (SA OC 428:8, first opinion, followed by the Rema).
7. **Joined parshiyos** read the second parsha's haftara (Rema OC 428:8). Exceptions:
   - Acharei Mos-Kedoshim: Ashkenazim read Acharei Mos's Amos 9:7-15 (Rema), and Chabad does too (CHB 2028-05-06). Sephardim read Kedoshim's Ezekiel 20:2-20 (EnWP).
   - Nitzavim-Vayelech reads Nitzavim's, the 7th consolation.
8. **Shabbos HaGadol.** The custom is about whether the special haftara is read at all; see `special.shabbos-hagadol`. When it is read, it replaces the weekly haftara (usually Tzav, sometimes Metzora or Acharei Mos).
9. **A bridegroom's haftara** ("Sos asis") never displaces any of the above (Rema OC 428:8; MB 425:12, 684:8). It is not encoded.

## Teimani: what was not found (left `null`)

- **Chayei Sarah:** whether the Baladi text is 1:1-31, 1:1-46, or 1:1-31 plus 1:46. The sources conflict.
- **Ki Sisa:** I Kings 18:1-45 (HeWP) or 18:1-46 (EnWP).
- **Masei / Matos-Masei / affliction-2:** Jeremiah 2:4-28 with 4:1-2 (EnWP), Isaiah 1:1-20 (HeWP; AHT for some Baladi), or Joshua 1:1-20 (EnWP citing HeWP).
- **Acharei Mos-Kedoshim and Behar-Bechukosai when joined:** not attested.
- **Sukkos II:** I Kings 7:51-8:16 (EnWP), 7:51-8:21 (HeWP) or 7:51-8:15 (AHT).
- **Shavuos II:** no Yemenite-specific statement found.
- **Tisha B'Av minchah:** Hosea 14:2-10 alone (HeWP; AHT for Baladi) or with Micah 7:18-20 (EnWP).
- **Shabbos HaGadol and fast-day minchah:** Baladi Yemenites read no special haftara (AHT, HeWP), so `null` here means "none" or "the weekly haftara".

## Other caveats

- The `chabad` entries for Vayakhel (I Kings 7:13-26) and the 1-Teves-only form of shabbos-chanukah-rosh-chodesh were not seen on a dated CHB page. Vayakhel rests on HeWP, AHT and EnWP. The Chanukah form is inferred from the verified Shekalim and HaChodesh Rosh Chodesh pattern.
- AHT lists Chabad's Shabbos Shuva as Hosea + Joel + Micah. CHB (twice) and HeWP give Hosea + Micah, and the JSON follows CHB.
- Israel-only differences (no second-day Yom Tov, Shushan Purim in Jerusalem, parsha offsets) are outside this file. The parsha haftaros themselves do not change between Israel and the Diaspora.

## Corrections (2026-10-21, later)

- **Kedoshim after a displaced Acharei Mos.** MB 428:26 (read on Sefaria) gives the reason for
  the Ashkenazi order: Kedoshim reads "Hatishpot" (Ezekiel 22) only because Acharei Mos already
  read "Halo kivnei kushiyim" (Amos 9); and, citing R. Akiva Eiger, when Acharei Mos fell on
  Erev Rosh Chodesh and read Machar Chodesh, Kedoshim reads Amos 9. An independent calendar library does the
  same (also after Shabbos HaGadol) and otherwise reads Amos on Acharei Mos and Ezekiel
  22:1-19 on Kedoshim: it does not swap them, as the Acharei Mos / Kedoshim bullet above
  implied. The only remaining difference with it is the end verse (22:16 or 22:19).
- **Shabbos Shuva (Ashkenaz).** MB 428:23 (read on Sefaria) records three customs (Micah,
  Joel, or both, as in Prague per the Eliyahu Rabba) and the Dagul Mervava's ruling: with
  Vayelech end with Micah 7:18-20, with Haazinu with Joel 2:15-27; it ends that all follows
  the custom. Luach now defaults to the Dagul Mervava (option `shuvaAddition`).
